Claude. ChatGPT. Gemini. Grok. Kimi. If you've ever opened three AI apps and wondered which one you're actually supposed to use, you're not alone.

The honest answer is that **no single AI is best at everything**. Each one is strongest at a different kind of work. Pick the right one for the job and you get better results in less time. Use one for everything and you get average work everywhere.

This is the full guide from my reel. For each job you'll find which AI to use, why, and a prompt you can copy straight in.

## The quick answer

| The job | Use this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Writing emails and proposals | **Claude** | Clear, natural writing that sounds like a person |
| Coding and fixing your website | **Claude** | Strong at real, multi-file code changes |
| Fact-checking with links | **Kimi** | Deep Research mode gives answers with sources |
| Posters and ads with text | **ChatGPT** | Puts readable, correctly spelled text in images |
| Video | **Veo** (in Google Flow) | Google's video model, with sound, up to 4K |
| Gmail and Google Docs | **Gemini** | Built right into your inbox and documents |

And if you were about to say Sora for video: OpenAI shut the Sora app down in April 2026. More on that below.

## For writing: use Claude

Proposals, client emails, website copy, social captions. Claude is the one most comparisons pick for writing, because the output reads like a person wrote it rather than a template.

**Try this:**

```text
Write a short, friendly email to a client who hasn't paid an invoice that's 14 days overdue. Keep it polite but clear, mention the invoice number [number] and the amount [amount], and offer to resend it. Under 120 words.
```

**Make it better:**

- Paste in two or three emails you've written yourself and ask it to match your tone.
- Say who's reading it ("a busy restaurant owner", "a corporate buyer"). The more it knows about the reader, the better it pitches the message.
- Ask for two versions, one softer and one firmer, and pick.

> Want real files, not just text? Claude can hand you a Word document, PowerPoint or Excel file when file creation is on. See [3 Claude prompts that do your business paperwork](/blog/3-claude-prompts-for-business-paperwork).

## For coding: use Claude again

Fixing a broken button, adding a contact form, building a landing page, explaining an error message. Claude ranks at or near the top of the main coding benchmarks in 2026 and is especially good at larger changes that touch several files at once.

**Try this:**

```text
My website's contact form button doesn't send anything when clicked. Here's the HTML and JavaScript: [paste code]. Find the problem, explain it in one sentence a non-developer would understand, and give me the fixed code.
```

**Make it better:**

- Paste the **exact error message**, not a description of it.
- Say what the site is built with (WordPress, Shopify, plain HTML, React) so the fix fits your setup.
- Test fixes on a copy of your site first, never straight on the live one.

## For fact-checking with links: use Kimi

When you need to know if something is actually true, and see where the answer came from, use Kimi. It's made by Moonshot AI, and its **Deep Research** mode searches across sources and gives you a report with citations you can click and check. It's available at [kimi.com](https://www.kimi.com).

**Try this:**

```text
Is sourdough bread still a growing trend for small bakeries in 2026? Give me a short yes-or-no answer first, then 3 key points, each with a source link I can check.
```

**Make it better:**

- Ask for the answer **first** and the details after. You'll know in five seconds whether to keep reading.
- Tell it which sources you trust ("government sites and major news only").
- Always click at least one link. Research tools can still get things wrong, and a source you've checked is worth ten you haven't.

> **Alternatives:** Perplexity is another strong research tool built around sourced answers, and ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all search the web with sources too. Kimi's edge is how thorough its Deep Research reports are.

## For posters and ads with text: use ChatGPT

AI images used to mangle words: "GRAND OPNEING", melted letters, random symbols. ChatGPT's image generator is the one to use when the words on the image matter, like a sale poster, a menu board or an Instagram ad. You can also ask for changes in plain English, like "make the 20% bigger".

**Try this:**

```text
Create a square Instagram poster for a bakery's grand opening. Big headline "GRAND OPENING", below it "20% OFF ALL WEEK", and the address "12 Mill Road" at the bottom. Warm, clean design with fresh bread in the background. Make sure all text is spelled exactly as written.
```

**Make it better:**

- Put every word you want on the image **in quotes**, exactly as it should appear.
- Keep text short. Three lines beats a paragraph.
- Give your brand colours as hex codes (for example `#3CFFA0`) instead of names like "green".

## For video: use Veo, not Sora

Here's the one that catches people out. **Sora is gone.** OpenAI announced in March 2026 that it was shutting Sora down. The app closed on 26 April 2026 and the developer API on 24 September 2026.

For video, use **Google's Veo 3.1**. It makes short clips with matching sound, and comparisons rate it the best all-round AI video model of 2026.

**Where to find it:** the simplest way to choose Veo by name is **Google Flow** ([labs.google/flow](https://labs.google/flow)), Google's AI video tool. It needs a Google AI plan. Video in the Gemini app now runs on Google's newer Gemini Omni model, so if you specifically want Veo, go to Flow.

**Try this:**

```text
An 8-second vertical video of a fresh sourdough loaf being sliced on a wooden board in a sunny bakery. Slow, close-up shot, steam rising from the bread. Sound: the crunch of the crust and soft café background noise.
```

**Make it better:**

- Describe it like a film director: **what's in the shot, how the camera moves, and what you hear**.
- Keep each clip to one simple action. Join clips together for longer videos.
- Ask for **vertical (9:16)** if it's for Reels, TikTok or Shorts.

## For Gmail and Google Docs: use Gemini

If your business runs on Google, Gemini is already sitting in your tools. In Gmail on a computer, click **Ask Gemini** at the top right to open the side panel. From there it can summarize long threads, draft replies, and find information in your old emails and your Drive files without you switching apps. It works in Google Docs, Sheets and Slides too.

**Try this (in the Gmail side panel):**

```text
Summarize the emails I got today in three bullet points: what needs a reply, what's a payment or invoice, and what can wait.
```

**Make it better:**

- Use the `@` mention in the side panel to point Gemini at a specific file from your Drive.
- Ask it to draft, then **you** check and press send. Gemini doesn't send emails for you.

> **Note:** the Gemini side panel isn't included in the free plan. You need an eligible Google AI or Google Workspace plan, and features vary by plan and country.

## What about Grok?

Grok is xAI's assistant and it lives inside X (formerly Twitter). Its strength is pulling from live posts on X, so it's useful for checking what people are saying about a topic right now. For the six everyday jobs above, the tools listed are a better fit.

## The rule to remember

Stop asking "which AI is best?" Ask **"what is it best at?"**

1. **Writing and coding:** Claude
2. **Fact-checking with links:** Kimi
3. **Posters and ads with text:** ChatGPT
4. **Video:** Veo in Google Flow
5. **Gmail and Google Docs:** Gemini

Whichever you use, check the output before it goes to a client: prices, dates, names and anything it says is a fact.
